Planning a wedding means deciding what to send and where the information should live. Two things are often confused: the invitation and the wedding website. They overlap, and some products now combine them, so it is fair to ask whether you need both. This guide explains what each one does, when a website is worth having, and three common ways to combine them.

## What each one does

An **invitation** is a personal request: it tells one guest (or one household) that they are invited, to what, when and where, and by when to reply. A **wedding website** is a reference: it holds everything guests may need to look up, and you can update it as plans change.

| | Invitation | Wedding website |
|---|---|---|
| Main job | Invite each guest personally | Answer guests' questions in one place |
| Sent to | Each invited guest or household | Shared by link or QR code with invited guests |
| What it says | Names, date, time, place, reply-by date | Schedule, map, dress code, travel, hotels, registry, FAQ |
| Can it change after sending? | Paper cannot; most digital cards cannot | Yes; guests always see the latest version |
| Formality | Sets the tone of the wedding | Practical; tone follows your design and wording |
| Replies | Reply card, or a link to reply online | Reply form, with your own questions |
| Registry | Never on the invitation | The usual place for it |

The [Emily Post Institute](https://emilypost.com/advice/wedding-website-tips) describes wedding websites as a useful place for travel and lodging information, updates and online replies, and suggests giving the address on an enclosure or reply card rather than on the invitation itself.

## Do you need a wedding website?

Not every wedding needs one. It earns its place when guests have more questions than an invitation can answer.

A website is worth it if any of these is true:

- [ ] Many guests travel, need hotels or come from another country
- [ ] There is more than one event (welcome drinks, ceremony, reception, brunch)
- [ ] You want replies in one list instead of texts, calls and cards
- [ ] You need to ask about meals, allergies or transport
- [ ] Details may change after the invitations go out
- [ ] You want guests to see your story and photos before the day

You can probably skip it if the wedding is small and local, everyone already knows the place, there is one event, and you are happy to collect replies by phone. In that case a clear invitation and a personal message may be enough.

## Do you still need an invitation if you have a website?

Yes. A website does not invite anyone on its own; it is a page that anyone with the link can open. The invitation is what tells a particular guest that they are expected, and who is included: "Sam and Alex" or "the Rivera family".

That is why sending only a website link with no personal message can feel cold. Even when the website does most of the work, the invitation step should be personal: a printed card, or a digital invitation sent individually with the guest's name. ## Three ways to combine them

**1. Paper invitation plus a wedding website.** The classic set-up. The invitation is printed and posted; a details card in the envelope gives the website address and a QR code; guests reply online. It suits formal weddings and guest lists with many older guests. Our guide on [putting your website on the invitation](/guides/wedding-website-on-invitation) has wording for each card.

**2. A digital invitation card plus a separate website.** An emailed or texted invitation card with its own reply button, and a separate website for the details. It works, but guests get two links, and replies may arrive in two places. Decide which one collects replies and say so clearly.

**3. One link: an invitation that opens into the website.** The guest receives one link, sees the invitation first (often as an envelope that opens) and then continues into the website with the details and the reply form. Everything is in one place, and changes reach everyone at once. Our guide to [animated envelope invitations](/guides/animated-envelope-wedding-invitations) explains how these work and what to check.

Many couples mix these: one link for most guests, and a simple printed card with the address and a QR code for those who prefer paper.

## What goes where

When both exist, keep each piece short and let it do its own job:

- **On the invitation:** your names (and the hosts, if traditional), the date and time, the venue and city, the reply-by date, and where to find the details.
- **On the website:** the schedule, the map and parking, the dress code, travel and hotels, the registry, answers to common questions, your story, and the reply form.
- **Only in a personal message:** anything private, such as a home address for an after-party or details for a small group.

Remember that a wedding website can be opened by anyone who has the link. Keep private addresses off it, and use a guest password if your platform offers one and you want the details limited to people you have told.

## A quick way to decide

Ask three questions:

1. **How formal is the wedding?** Formal: paper invitation plus website. Relaxed: one link is usually enough.
2. **How much will guests need to look up?** A lot: you need a website, whatever form the invitation takes.
3. **Who will send the invitations?** If you want a service to send and track them for you, choose a digital card. If you are happy to send one link yourself, an invitation that opens into the website keeps everything together.

## FAQ

### Can a wedding website replace invitations?

Not on its own. A website holds details, but it does not personally invite anyone or say who is included. Send each guest an invitation, printed or digital, that points to the website. An invitation that opens into the website does both jobs with one link.

### Is it rude to send only a link to our wedding website?

A bare link with no message can feel impersonal. Send each guest a short personal message with their name, a line inviting them, and the link. If the link opens as an invitation first, the guest still has the feeling of being invited rather than sent a web page.

### Do small weddings need a wedding website?

Often not. If the wedding is local, there is one event and everyone knows the place, a clear invitation and replies by phone or message may be enough. A website becomes useful once guests travel, there are several events or you need answers about meals.

### Should RSVPs go on the invitation or the website?

Choose one place. Most couples now collect replies on the website and print the reply-by date and the address on the invitation suite. Two ways to reply means two lists to merge and more chances to miss someone.
