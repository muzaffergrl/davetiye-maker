Digital invitations are no longer unusual. They are fast, easy to update and kinder to a budget. But a wedding invitation is also a small piece of etiquette, and some guests will notice how it arrives. This guide gives you both sides honestly, so you can decide what suits your guest list.

## Is it acceptable to send a digital wedding invitation?

For most weddings and most guests, yes. Traditional etiquette is more careful: the [Emily Post Institute](https://emilypost.com/advice/wedding-email-dos-and-donts) still prefers a printed invitation for a wedding because of its personal nature, while describing [e-vites as a good fit](https://emilypost.com/advice/wedding-evites) for weddings planned at short notice and for pre-wedding events such as showers.

What this means in practice:

- For a relaxed, modern or small wedding, a well-designed digital invitation sent personally is a perfectly gracious choice.
- For a very formal wedding, or a family where tradition matters, paper invitations (with the website for details and replies) may be the better fit.
- For almost every wedding, a mix works: digital for most guests, paper for the few who prefer it.

The way you send it matters more than the medium. A personal message to each guest with a beautiful link feels thoughtful; a link dropped into a large group chat does not.

## Pros and cons

| | Digital invitation | Printed invitation |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Usually a one-time price or free; no postage | Printing, envelopes and postage per guest |
| Speed | Arrives instantly; easy to send to guests abroad | Needs printing and mailing time |
| Changes | Details can be corrected after sending | A mistake means reprinting or a correction card |
| Replies | Guests reply online; replies are counted for you | Reply cards to mail back and tally by hand |
| Keepsake | Nothing to hold, though screenshots and the website remain | A physical keepsake many guests keep |
| Formality | Can feel less formal to some guests | Traditional and clearly formal |
| Reach | Needs a phone or computer and an internet connection | Reaches everyone with a postal address |

## Who might still need paper

Think through your guest list and set aside a short list for paper:

- Grandparents and older relatives who do not use smartphones or rarely check email
- Guests who have told you they love a printed invitation
- Very formal relationships: a partner's boss, family friends of your parents
- Anyone without reliable internet access

A simple printed card with your names, the date and a QR code that opens your website gives them the same details and the same reply form. Scan the QR code from a printed proof with two different phones before you print the full batch.

## How to send a digital invitation

**Send it one by one.** Message each guest or couple individually, from your own number or email. It takes longer, but it is the single biggest difference between thoughtful and impersonal.

**Choose the channel they actually use.** Text or WhatsApp for most friends, email for colleagues and older guests who prefer it. Avoid sending the only copy through a social network.

**Check the preview before you send.** Most messaging apps show a preview card for a link: an image, a title and a short description. Send the link to yourself first. If the preview shows your names and the date, guests know at a glance what it is. If it shows something generic, guests may mistake it for spam. Our guide to [wedding website privacy](/guides/wedding-website-privacy) explains what a forwarded preview reveals.

**Address the people who are invited.** If the invitation is for one person or a couple only, say their names in your message. If children or a plus-one are invited, say so.

**Send everything at once.** Invitations sent over several days lead to awkward "did you get yours?" conversations. Set an evening aside and send them together.

## Wording for the message you send with the link

Short, warm and clear about the action you need. Adapt these:

**Warm:**
*"Hi Maya! We're getting married on June 12, 2027 in Napa, and we would love for you to be there. Here is our invitation: (link). You can reply on the website by May 15. Love, Clara & Julian"*

**Classic:**
*"Dear Mr. and Mrs. Patel, together with our families, we would be honored by your presence at our wedding on Saturday, June 12, 2027. Our invitation and all details are here: (link). Kindly reply by May 15. With warm regards, Clara Rose and Julian Thomas"*

**For a couple:**
*"Hi Sam and Alex! We'd love you both to celebrate with us on June 12. Everything is here, including the reply form: (link)."*

**When children are invited:**
*"Hi Jo! The whole family is invited, little ones included. When you reply, let us know their ages so we can plan meals: (link)."*

**With a plus-one:**
*"Hi Leo! We'd love you to join us on June 12, and you're welcome to bring a guest. When you reply, just add their name: (link)."*

**If a detail changes after you have sent it:**
*"Hi Maya, a quick update: the ceremony now starts at 4:30 pm instead of 4:00 pm. Everything else stays the same, and the website always has the latest details: (link)."*

**A save the date, months before the invitation:**
*"Hi Sam! Save the date: we're getting married on June 12, 2027 in Napa. A formal invitation will follow, and our website already has the first details: (link)."*

## Making a digital invitation feel personal

Small choices make a digital invitation feel as considered as a paper one:

- **Use names, not "Hi all".** Even a short message reads very differently when it starts with the guest's name.
- **Add one personal line** for close friends and family: a shared memory, or "we couldn't imagine the day without you".
- **Send from your own number or email,** not from a platform address guests do not recognize.
- **Pick a calm moment.** An evening message is more likely to be opened and read properly than one sent during the working day.
- **Make the design count.** A link that opens on something beautiful, rather than a plain form, sets the tone for the day.

## Following up with guests who have not replied

A week before your reply-by date, check your list and send a gentle note to anyone missing:

*"Hi Tom, just a friendly reminder that we're finalizing numbers for the caterer. Could you reply by Friday? Here's the link again: (link). Thank you!"*

After the deadline, call the last few guests. Older relatives in particular may have opened the link and not found the reply button; a quick call solves it. ## FAQ

### Are digital wedding invitations tacky?

Not when they are well designed and sent personally. Etiquette experts still lean towards paper for formal weddings, but digital invitations are widely used and accepted. Sending each invitation individually, with a warm message, is what keeps it gracious.

### Can we send save the dates digitally and invitations on paper?

Yes, and many couples do. Digital save the dates are quick and can link to your website early; printed invitations then carry the formality. Make sure everyone who receives a save the date also receives an invitation.

### Should we send digital invitations by email or text?

Use the channel each guest actually reads. Text or WhatsApp works for most friends; email suits colleagues and older guests. Whatever you choose, send individually rather than to a group, and check the link preview first.

### Is it rude to only accept online RSVPs?

It is fine for most guests, as long as you make an exception for anyone who cannot use it. Let older relatives reply by phone or in person and note their answer on your list yourself, or ask a family member to help them.
