Paper invitations have a small moment that digital ones usually lose: the envelope. You see your name, break the seal and slide the card out. An animated envelope invitation brings that moment back to a phone screen. This guide explains what these invitations are, how guests actually experience them, the two kinds you will find, and what to check before you pay for one.

## What is an animated envelope invitation?

An animated envelope invitation is a digital wedding invitation that first appears as a closed envelope. When a guest taps it, the envelope opens on screen: a seal lifts or a ribbon comes undone, the flap folds back and the invitation card rises out. After that, the guest reads the invitation and, in most cases, replies online.

The animation is the wrapping, not the content. What matters afterwards is the same as for any invitation: the names, the date, the place, the reply-by date and an easy way to answer.

Not every design uses an envelope. The same idea works with other keepsakes that open: a folded gatefold card, a small pouch, a travel wallet, a storybook or a vellum sleeve. What they share is a first screen that invites one tap, and a reveal that feels like opening something.

## How guests open it

For a guest, the experience usually looks like this:

1. They receive a message from you (text, WhatsApp or email) with a link. The link preview shows a picture and a title.
2. They tap the link, and the envelope fills their phone screen. Nothing happens until they tap again.
3. They tap the envelope. It opens, piece by piece, and the invitation appears.
4. They read the invitation and continue: to the details, to the reply form, or both.

Three details make a real difference on a phone:

- **The link preview.** Messaging apps build the preview from the page's share tags (the [Open Graph](https://ogp.me/) title and image). A good preview shows your names or a picture of the envelope, not a generic logo.
- **Reduced motion.** Some guests turn on "reduce motion" in their phone settings because animation makes them dizzy. The [W3C's accessibility guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html) recommends respecting that setting for animation that a tap starts. A careful design shows a gentle fade instead, and the guest still reaches the invitation.
- **A way past the animation.** Guests who come back a second time to check the time or the address should not have to wait through the whole opening again.

## Two kinds: an emailed card or an envelope that opens into a website

Search for animated envelope invitations and you will see two quite different products under the same name. Both are valid; they suit different couples.

| | Emailed invitation card | Envelope that opens into a wedding website |
|---|---|---|
| What the guest gets | A card, often addressed to them by name | A website: the envelope first, then pages for the day |
| How it is sent | Usually sent by the service, to an email or phone list you upload | You share one link yourself, by text, WhatsApp or email |
| Where details live | On the card, or on a separate wedding website you link to | On the website itself: schedule, map, dress code, travel |
| RSVP | A reply button on the card | A reply form on the website |
| Changing a detail later | Depends on the service; sent cards may not update | Update the website; everyone sees the change on the same link |
| Typical pricing model | Often priced per guest or per send | Often a single price per design or per website |
| Tracking who opened it | Often included | Usually not; you see who replied |

Two questions usually decide it:

- **Do you want the service to send the invitations for you?** If yes, and open tracking matters to you, an emailed card is built for that.
- **Do you also need a wedding website?** If yes, an envelope that opens into the website gives you one link for everything, instead of a card plus a second site. Our guide on [what to put on your wedding website](/guides/what-to-put-on-your-wedding-website) shows what that site usually needs.

## What to check before you choose

Whichever kind you pick, check these before paying. Most can be tested on a demo in a few minutes.

- [ ] Open the demo on your own phone, not only on a computer
- [ ] Turn on "reduce motion" on your phone and open it again: you should still reach the invitation
- [ ] Send the demo link to yourself in WhatsApp or Messages and look at the preview
- [ ] Check that guests need no app, account or download to open it or to reply
- [ ] Find where replies go, and whether you can download them as a spreadsheet
- [ ] Find out what you can change after you share it, and what costs extra
- [ ] Find out how long the link stays online after the wedding
- [ ] Check whether a guest password is available, if you do not want the details public

If the opening takes more than a few seconds, or the reply form is hard to find after it, guests will notice. A beautiful opening should lead quickly to the useful part.

## Is an animated invitation formal enough?

Traditional etiquette still treats the printed invitation as the most formal choice. The [Emily Post Institute](https://emilypost.com/advice/wedding-evites) describes digital invitations as practical and well suited to weddings planned at short notice, while preferring paper for the invitation itself in most cases. It also suggests sending printed details to guests who do not use the internet much.

In practice, many couples now combine the two: an animated invitation for most guests, and a simple printed card with the website address or a QR code for those who prefer paper. Send each invitation personally rather than to a group chat. ## Sound, length and other details

A few smaller choices are worth a moment:

- **Music should never start by surprise.** Many guests open invitations in quiet places. If a design has music, it is best when the guest starts it.
- **Keep the envelope readable.** Your names or a monogram on the envelope tell guests what they are about to open.
- **Check it in dark mode and in bright sunlight.** Pale gold text on ivory can disappear on a phone outdoors.
- **Mind the file size.** A heavy animation on a slow connection shows a blank screen. Try the demo once on mobile data, not only on Wi-Fi.

## FAQ

### Do guests need an app to open an animated envelope invitation?

They should not. A well-made animated invitation opens in the phone's normal browser from a link in a text, WhatsApp message or email. Before you choose, open the demo yourself from a message, and check that replying does not ask guests to create an account.

### Can I send an animated envelope invitation by WhatsApp?

Yes, if the invitation is a link. Send it to each guest individually with a short personal line, and check the link preview first by sending it to yourself. If the invitation is a card that a service emails for you, it may need email addresses instead.

### What if a guest finds the animation distracting?

Guests who turn on reduced motion on their phone should still reach the invitation, with a calmer transition. Check this on a demo before you choose. Guests who come back later should be able to go straight to the details without waiting for the opening again.

### Can an animated invitation replace a wedding website?

It can if it opens into one. An emailed card usually holds only the invitation, so details like the schedule, travel and dress code still need a website. An envelope that opens into a website gives guests one link for the invitation, the details and the reply.
