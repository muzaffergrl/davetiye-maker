Every wedding has guests who do not reply on time. Usually it is not rudeness: the invitation went to an old email, the link was opened on the bus and forgotten, or the guest is waiting to hear about time off. A calm, planned follow-up gets almost everyone answered without awkwardness. This guide shows how to find out who is missing, what to send and when, and what to do with the last few.

## Why guests don't reply

Knowing the reason helps you pick the right message:

- **It never arrived.** An old email address, a message in a group chat that scrolled away, a card lost in the post.
- **They forgot.** They opened it, meant to answer later and never did.
- **They are not sure yet.** Waiting for time off, a babysitter or flight prices.
- **They did not realize a reply was needed.** Especially guests who rarely get formal invitations.
- **Something did not work.** A link that did not open, a form that confused them, a password they lost.
- **They assumed someone else replied.** One person in a couple or family thought the other had done it.

Most of these are solved by one personal message that makes replying easy.

## First, find out who is missing

Before you send anything, make one clear list of who has not answered:

- [ ] Download your replies the day you start following up
- [ ] Put them next to your guest list, one line per invited household
- [ ] Match replies to households, not to people: one reply often covers a couple or a family
- [ ] Watch for spelling differences ("Kate" and "Katherine" may be the same guest)
- [ ] Check messages, calls and paper cards for replies that never reached the form, and add them to your list
- [ ] Mark everyone left as "no reply yet", with how you invited them

Doing this once, carefully, avoids the worst follow-up mistake: reminding someone who has already replied.

## A follow-up plan

| When | What to do | How |
|---|---|---|
| About 1 week before the deadline | A friendly reminder to everyone who has not replied | A short personal message with the link |
| The day after the deadline | One more message, a little more direct | Personal message, mentioning the caterer's date |
| 2 to 3 days after the deadline | Call the last few | Phone, or ask a family member who knows them |
| The day you give final numbers | Decide how to count anyone still silent, and tell them | A kind, clear message |

The [Emily Post Institute](https://emilypost.com/advice/what-to-do-when-guests-dont-rsvp) is clear that it is perfectly polite to call guests who have not responded, and that a friendly tone matters more than the exact words. It also suggests asking family or the wedding party to help with calls. Set your deadline with this week of follow-up in mind; our guide on [choosing the RSVP deadline](/guides/wedding-rsvp-deadline) shows how.

## Messages you can send

Keep each one short, personal and easy to act on. Start with the guest's name, and include the link every time.

**A week before the deadline**

*"Hi Sam! Just a gentle reminder that replies for our wedding are due by May 21. It only takes a minute: (your link). We'd love to know if you can make it."*

**The day after the deadline**

*"Hi Sam, we're giving our caterer final numbers this week and haven't heard from you yet. Could you let us know by Wednesday if you can come? Here's the link again: (your link). Thank you!"*

**If the link may have failed**

*"Hi Sam, we're checking that our invitation reached you: here it is again (your link). If anything doesn't open, just reply here with yes or no and we'll add you ourselves."*

**The last message, when you must decide**

*"Hi Sam, we have to confirm numbers with the caterer today, so we'll assume you can't make it this time. If that's wrong, please call us before Friday and we'll do our best. We hope to celebrate with you soon either way."*

**A phone call, in a few lines**

*"Hi, it's Clara. I'm calling about the wedding invitation, just to check it reached you. We're giving the caterer final numbers on Friday. Do you think you'll be able to come?"*

Keep reminders out of group chats. A public reminder embarrasses the people who have not replied and annoys everyone who has. Our guide to [digital wedding invitation etiquette](/guides/digital-wedding-invitation-etiquette) has more on sending messages individually.

## When you have to decide

At some point you need a number. If a guest is still silent on the day your caterer needs final counts, you have two fair choices:

- **Count them as not attending**, and tell them so in a kind message (above). This protects your budget and your seating plan.
- **Count them as attending**, if you know them well and they are very likely to come. You pay for a meal that might not be eaten, but avoid an awkward moment.

Whichever you choose, make the decision once and stick to it. If an invitation said "regrets only", the rule is reversed: silence means the guest is coming.

## Late replies and unexpected guests

- **A late "yes":** thank them, then check with your caterer and venue before confirming. Some can still add a person or two after the final count; ask early what their limit is.
- **A late "no":** thank them and update your list. If you already gave numbers, ask the caterer whether the count can still change.
- **Someone arrives who never replied:** greet them warmly and find a seat. Ask the venue in advance whether they can keep one or two spare places and meals for this.
- **A guest brings someone who was not invited:** deal with it quietly and kindly on the day. Address the invitation precisely next time; our guide to [wedding RSVP questions](/guides/wedding-rsvp-questions) explains how to word the reply form so the number in each reply is clear.

## Making replies easier next time

Fewer reminders are needed when replying is easy:

- Put the reply-by date in full on every card and on the website.
- Use one way to reply, not two.
- Make the form short: whether the guest is coming, how many people, and only the questions someone will use.
- Test the link from a message on your own phone before you send invitations.
- Add a QR code to printed cards for guests who do not type addresses; see [how to put your wedding website on the invitation](/guides/wedding-website-on-invitation).

## FAQ

### Is it rude to remind wedding guests to RSVP?

No. A short, friendly reminder sent personally is normal and expected, and etiquette guides say calling guests who have not replied is polite. Avoid public reminders in group chats, and always include the link so replying takes a minute.

### How many times should I remind guests to RSVP?

Usually twice: once about a week before the deadline and once the day after it. Then call the last few. More messages rarely help; a phone call or a word from a family member works better than a third text.

### Should I count guests who did not RSVP as coming?

Decide case by case, then stick to it. Counting them as not attending protects your budget; counting close family as attending avoids awkwardness. Tell the guest what you decided, kindly, before you give the caterer your final numbers.

### What do I say to a guest who never replied but wants to come?

Thank them, say you will check, and ask your caterer and venue whether one more is possible. If it is, confirm warmly. If not, explain honestly that numbers were already final, and find another way to celebrate with them.
