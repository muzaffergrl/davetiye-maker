Free wedding websites are genuinely good for many couples. Paid ones are not automatically better; they sell something specific, and it is worth knowing what. This guide sorts the options by how you pay, lists what each usually includes, points out the costs that are easy to miss, and gives you questions to ask before you commit.

## The short answer

A free wedding website is enough if you want a clear page with your details and online replies, and you do not mind a standard design or a web address on the platform's domain. Paying makes sense when something specific matters to you: a particular look, your own domain name, no third-party branding, or features a free plan leaves out.

The more useful question is not "free or paid?" but "what exactly am I paying for, and for how long?"

## Four ways wedding websites are priced

| Model | What you pay | Usually included | Check before choosing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free, from a wedding planning platform | Nothing for the site | Templates, reply form, registry links, guest messages | Ads or branding, what is paid extra, how your guests' data is used |
| Free plan of a general website builder | Nothing, with limits | A basic site on the builder's address | Builder ads, page limits, whether forms and passwords need an upgrade |
| Monthly or yearly plan | A recurring fee | Design freedom, your own domain, no ads | Total cost to the wedding and after, auto-renewal, cancelling |
| One-time purchase | A single price | A designed site for a set period | How long it stays online, what you can change later, what is not included |

None of these is right for everyone. A couple who wants planning tools in one place may prefer a free platform; a couple who cares most about how the invitation looks may prefer a paid design.

## What free plans usually include

Free wedding sites have improved a lot. Most let you:

- Publish your names, date, venue, schedule and travel details
- Collect replies online with a few questions
- Link to one or more gift registries
- Choose from a set of ready-made designs
- Share a link and sometimes a QR code

What you often trade for "free" is control: a design shared by many couples, the platform's name in your web address or footer, and occasional promotion of the platform's own services. Read what is included in the free plan and what is offered as an upgrade, so you are not surprised halfway through.

## What paying usually adds

Paid options tend to sell one or more of these:

- **Design.** Artwork, typography and animation you will not see on every other wedding site.
- **Your own web address.** A custom domain such as your names, instead of a sub-address on the platform.
- **No ads or third-party branding.**
- **More control.** Extra pages, custom fonts or code on general website builders.
- **Planning tools.** Guest lists, seating plans and email tracking on some paid plans.

If none of these matter to you, a free site is a sensible choice.

## Costs that are easy to miss

- **Custom domains renew.** A domain name is registered for a period and must be renewed before it expires to keep working; [ICANN's guidance for domain owners](https://www.icann.org/resources/pages/domain-name-renewal-expiration-faqs-2018-12-07-en) explains the renewal reminders and grace periods. Budget for at least one renewal if your wedding is more than a year away.
- **Monthly plans add up.** Multiply the monthly price by the months from today until a few weeks after the wedding. Then check whether the plan renews automatically.
- **Add-ons inside a free plan.** Password protection, extra pages or removing branding may be paid upgrades.
- **Printed extras.** A free site does not include printed invitations or postage; budget for them separately if you need paper.
- **Your time.** A general website builder can do anything, but someone has to build it. A ready-made wedding design takes far less time.

## Questions to ask before you choose

Use this list for any option, free or paid:

- [ ] How long does the website stay online after the wedding, and what happens then?
- [ ] Is anything charged again: monthly, yearly or when a domain renews?
- [ ] Can guests open the site and reply without creating an account?
- [ ] Can you download the replies as a spreadsheet?
- [ ] Can you add a guest password if you want the details private?
- [ ] What can you change after you share the link, and is any change limited or paid?
- [ ] Does the platform show ads or its own branding on your site?
- [ ] Can you try a live demo on your phone before you pay?

## When a free website is the better choice

- You want registry, planning and the website in one place.
- Your wedding is simple, and a standard design is fine.
- Budget is tight and every saving counts.
- You will build the site yourself and enjoy doing it.

## When paying makes sense

- The look of the invitation matters to you, and you want something guests have not seen many times.
- You want your own web address, or no third-party branding.
- You want a fixed price with nothing to renew before the wedding.
- You would rather fill in a finished design than build pages.

## FAQ

### Are free wedding websites really free?

Usually yes for the basics: your details, a reply form and registry links. Some features may be paid upgrades, such as a custom domain, removing branding or password protection, and some free sites show the platform's own promotions. Read the free plan's list before you start.

### Is a custom domain worth it for a wedding website?

It is a nice touch, not a necessity. A custom domain is easier to say aloud and looks tidy on printed cards, but it costs money and must be renewed. A short address with your names on a platform's domain works just as well for most guests.

### Do I have to pay for a wedding website every year?

Only with recurring plans or a custom domain. Monthly and yearly plans renew until you cancel, and domain names must be renewed to keep working. Free sites and one-time purchases have nothing to renew, but check how long a one-time purchase stays online.

### What happens to a wedding website after the wedding?

It depends on the platform. Some keep the site online indefinitely, some for a set period, and some until you stop paying. Ask before you choose, and download your replies and any photos you want to keep well before the site goes offline.
